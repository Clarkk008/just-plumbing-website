Identify Your Pipe Material

From 1980s polybutylene to post-war galvanized steel, failing pipes put your property at risk of catastrophic water damage and insurance cancellation. Select your piping system below to learn its failure mechanisms and replacement protocol.

Polybutylene (Quest) Pipes

Installed widely from 1978–1995. Municipal water disinfectants break down the plastic resin and acetal fittings from within, causing sudden, catastrophic wall and ceiling bursts.

  • Identified By: Flexible dull-gray pipe stamped "PB2110"
  • Primary Risk: Sudden fitting blowout & insurance dropped policies
  • Common In: Highlands Ranch, Parker, Centennial
Read Polybutylene Guide →

Galvanized Steel Piping

Found throughout pre-1960s post-war homes. Internal rust and calcium accumulation restrict water volume to a crawl, produce discolored water flakes, and corrode threaded joints.

  • Identified By: Silver-gray rigid metallic pipe with threaded fittings
  • Primary Risk: Severe pressure loss & rust contamination
  • Common In: Denver, Englewood, Wheat Ridge, Edgewater
Read Galvanized Guide →

Kitec Composite Piping

Installed from 1995–2007. Prone to severe dezincification of yellow brass fittings (white powdery crust) and pipe delamination under Front Range municipal water pressure.

  • Identified By: Bright orange (hot) and bright blue (cold) pipes
  • Primary Risk: Brass fitting dezincification & pipe rupture
  • Common In: Thornton, Westminster, Broomfield
Read Kitec Guide →

Copper Pinhole Leaks

Pitting corrosion from aggressive water chemistry, soil friction beneath slabs, and excessive line velocity eats microscopic holes through copper walls behind drywall and under floors.

  • Identified By: Green crusting patina on copper lines and damp drywall
  • Primary Risk: Subterranean slab leaks & ceiling rot
  • Common In: Arvada, Lakewood, Littleton, Sheridan

Frequently Asked Questions — Pipe Diagnostics

How do I identify defective polybutylene pipes in my home?

Polybutylene pipes are typically flexible, dull gray (sometimes blue or black) plastic lines stamped with code 'PB2110'. They were widely installed between 1978 and 1995 and are frequently visible at water heater connections, under sink cabinets, or near the main water meter.

What causes copper pinhole leaks in Denver Metro plumbing?

Copper pinhole leaks are primarily caused by excessive municipal water velocity, high line pressure (above 80 PSI), aggressive water chemistry, and mechanical friction along subterranean lines installed directly beneath concrete foundation slabs.

Can defective plumbing lines be replaced without tearing down all the walls?

Yes. Master Plumber Bill Clark specializes in minimally invasive repiping. By threading flexible Uponor PEX-A expansion piping through basement floor joists, drop ceilings, crawlspaces, and interior partition cavities, we replace whole-home supply systems with drywall openings in just 1 to 2 days.
