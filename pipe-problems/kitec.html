The Recall Crisis of Kitec Plumbing Along the Front Range

Manufactured by IPEX between 1995 and 2007, Kitec plumbing was widely marketed as a corrosion-proof, cost-effective alternative to copper. Built as an aluminum core sandwiched between inner and outer layers of cross-linked polyethylene (PEX-AL-PEX), Kitec was aggressively installed across thousands of homes built in the late 1990s and 2000s in Thornton, Westminster, Broomfield, Commerce City, and Brighton.

By 2005, premature failures led to a full recall and a landmark multi-million-dollar class-action settlement. In Colorado, the combination of municipal water chemistry, thermal cycling, and high line pressure accelerates the structural failure of both the composite pipe and its accompanying brass fittings.

The Two Fatal Flaws of Kitec Plumbing Systems

1. Yellow Brass Dezincification ("Meringue" Corrosion)

The primary vulnerability in Kitec systems lies in its high-zinc yellow brass fittings. When exposed to municipal water, a chemical reaction leaches the zinc out of the alloy. This process, known as dezincification, leaves behind a brittle, porous copper matrix while generating a white, powdery, meringue-like zinc oxide deposit on the fitting exterior. As the white powder expands internally, it chokes off water pressure. Eventually, the structural integrity vanishes, causing the fitting to shear off cleanly under normal municipal pressure.

2. Composite Pipe Delamination & Blistering

The aluminum layer inside Kitec pipe expands and contracts at a different rate than the inner and outer polyethylene layers. Under continuous hot water temperatures and pressure spikes, the adhesive bonding layers fail. The pipe delaminates, forming bubbles and blisters along the exterior wall that eventually rupture, flooding finished basements and ceilings.

Real Estate Disclosures & Homeowner Insurance Denials

Why Kitec Cannot Be Repaired with Standard Fittings

Homeowners often ask if a leaking Kitec elbow can simply be patched. The answer is an emphatic no. Kitec was manufactured to unique imperial/metric composite dimensions that do not match standard ASTM PEX pipe diameters. Standard PEX crimps, expansion rings, and push-fit connectors (like SharkBites) cannot create a safe or code-approved seal on Kitec tubing.

Furthermore, because dezincification affects every fitting in the house simultaneously, repairing one joint leaves dozens of compromised fittings waiting to burst. Our Master Plumber team performs turnkey whole-home repiping using scale-proof Uponor PEX-A expansion piping (backed by a 25-year manufacturer material warranty and our 5-year workmanship guarantee) or commercial-grade Type L rigid copper. Frequently Asked Questions — Kitec Pipe Replacement

How do I identify Kitec plumbing in my Colorado home?

Kitec is typically identified by its distinctive bright orange (hot water) and bright blue (cold water) flexible composite piping, although some installations used gray, black, or white tubing. Look for stampings like 'Kitec', 'KTC', 'PlumbBetter', or 'ASTM F1281'. It is also characterized by yellow brass crimp fittings, often marked with 'Kitec' or 'KTC'.

What is dezincification in Kitec brass fittings?

Dezincification is a chemical reaction where water leaches zinc out of the high-zinc yellow brass fittings. This leaves behind a weakened, brittle copper sponge and produces a white, chalky, meringue-like zinc oxide deposit on the fitting exterior that restricts water flow and leads to catastrophic fitting shear.

Why is Kitec plumbing a roadblock for real estate transactions and home insurance?

Because Kitec was subject to an extensive nationwide class action recall due to high failure rates, home inspectors flag it on inspection reports. Major Colorado insurance companies regularly refuse to write new homeowner policies or exclude water damage on homes with active Kitec, causing mortgage lenders to delay or deny loan funding.

Can Kitec pipes be repaired or patched locally?

No. Kitec pipe dimensions are non-standard and cannot be safely joined with standard PEX crimps or push-to-connect fittings. Furthermore, because dezincification and pipe delamination occur throughout the entire plumbing network, isolated patches only transfer stress to other degrading fittings. Whole-home repiping is the only permanent, code-compliant fix.
