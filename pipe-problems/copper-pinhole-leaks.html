Why Copper Pipes Develop Pinhole Leaks in Colorado

1. Pitting Corrosion from Water Chemistry

Aggressive mineral content, hard water, and pH variations cause localized galvanic reactions on the interior wall of thin copper pipes, eating away microscopic pinholes that rupture under normal pressure.

2. Excessive Municipal Water Pressure

Many Front Range municipal supply lines enter homes at over 80–100 PSI. This excessive velocity creates internal turbulence that erodes copper elbows and pipe fittings from the inside out.

3. Under-Slab Chemical Contact

Copper pipes embedded in concrete slabs or gravel beds interact with moisture and chemical salts in the soil, leading to catastrophic slab leaks that can compromise foundations.

4. Permanent Replacement with Uponor PEX-A

Patching pinholes in compromised copper is only a temporary fix before another leak forms nearby. We replace degraded copper systems with full-flow Uponor PEX-A expansion piping (with a 25-year material warranty and 5-year workmanship warranty) or thick-walled Type L rigid copper where metallic mechanical transitions are required.
