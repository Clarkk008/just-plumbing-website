West Highland (Denver) Ranch Home Repipe

The Challenge: Mixed Materials and Crippled Water Flow

Located in the vibrant West Highland neighborhood of Denver, this classic 1954 mid-century ranch was exhibiting classic signs of end-of-life plumbing. The homeowner reported that hot water took several minutes to reach the kitchen sink, and the shower pressure in the main bathroom was practically non-existent. Furthermore, they had discovered small, green "weeping" spots on exposed pipes inside the crawlspace.

During the consultation, Master Plumber Bill Clark evaluated the system and found a disastrous mix of materials. The home's main trunks were original galvanized steel, heavily clogged with rust. Over the years, handymen had spliced in early-generation thin-wall copper, which was now suffering from pinhole leaks due to galvanic corrosion where the dissimilar metals met.

The Master Plumber Diagnosis & Crawlspace Strategy

One of the homeowner's primary concerns was protecting their beautifully restored original oak hardwood floors. Many plumbers opt for the fastest route, which often involves cutting up floors to access pipes. Bill engineered a "bottom-up" routing plan: our team would utilize the home's 3-foot crawlspace to run the new distribution lines, drilling precisely up through the bottom wall plates directly into the partition cavities.

Additionally, Bill measured the incoming city water pressure at a dangerously high 95 PSI. To protect the new system and the home's appliances, a Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) and thermal expansion tank were mandated as part of the installation.

The 1.5-Day Execution Timeline

Day 1: PRV Integration & Crawlspace Routing

Work began by shutting off the main service line and installing a new PRV, calibrated to a safe 65 PSI. Our crew entered the crawlspace, draining and safely abandoning the heavy, rusted galvanized pipes. We strung flexible Uponor PEX-A lines along the floor joists, heavily insulating the hot water lines to reduce heat loss and speed up hot water delivery to the kitchen. By the end of day one, the new main lines were pressurized and active.

Day 2: Vertical Feeds & Professional Drywall Patching

Our team made minimal cuts behind the bathroom vanities, toilets, and shower valves. We drilled up from the crawlspace, feeding the 1/2-inch PEX-A lines directly to the fixtures. Every connection utilized full-port ProPEX expansion rings, ensuring zero restriction to the newly restored water volume. Following a successful Denver city inspection, our dedicated drywall professional patched, mudded, and textured all access holes seamlessly.

The Danger of Galvanic Corrosion

When copper piping is connected directly to galvanized steel without a proper dielectric union, an electrochemical process called galvanic corrosion occurs. The water acts as an electrolyte, causing the steel to rust aggressively and the copper to deteriorate rapidly at the joint.

In this home, improper handyman splices were essentially "eating" the pipes from the inside out. By replacing the entire mixed-material system with non-metallic, inert Uponor PEX-A, we permanently eliminated the risk of electrochemical pipe failure.

The Outcome

In just a day and a half, the West Highland homeowner regained silent, high-volume water pressure. Hot water delivery to the kitchen was significantly faster due to the insulated PEX routing. The original hardwood floors remained completely untouched, and the home was secured with a 25-year manufacturer material warranty.

Frequently Asked Questions — Mid-Century Repiping

Why did my 1950s ranch home suddenly lose water pressure?

Homes built in the 1950s often used a mix of galvanized steel mains and early thin-wall copper branches. Over decades, the galvanized steel rusts internally, creating thick mineral nodules called tuberculation. This chokes the inner diameter of the pipe, drastically reducing water pressure and volume to your fixtures.

Do you have to tear up my hardwood floors to repipe a home with a crawlspace?

No. When repiping a home with a crawlspace or unfinished basement, our Master Plumber team routes the new Uponor PEX-A lines beneath the main floor. We precisely drill up through the bottom wall plates directly into the partition cavities, keeping your finished hardwood and tile floors completely intact.

Why do you install a PRV (Pressure Reducing Valve) during a repipe?

Denver's municipal water pressure can fluctuate wildly, sometimes spiking over 100 PSI. High pressure damages appliance valves, causes pipe hammer, and voids fixture warranties. We install a calibrated PRV to regulate incoming water to a safe, steady 65 PSI, protecting your new plumbing investment.
