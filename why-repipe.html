Why Repipe Your Home?

★★★★☆ 4.6 Stars based on 68 Google Reviews

Don't wait for a catastrophic flood. Learn the warning signs of failing Denver plumbing systems and why upgrading to PEX-A or Copper is the smartest investment you can make.

The 4 Major Signs Your Pipes Are Failing

💧

Low Water Pressure

If you can't run the dishwasher and take a shower at the same time, your pipes are likely choked with years of mineral scale and internal rust buildup.

🚰

Rusty or Foul Water

Does your tap water look orange or brown when you first turn it on? That means your metal pipes are decaying and flaking rust directly into your drinking water.

⚠️

Constant Pinhole Leaks

One leak is an accident. Multiple pinhole leaks or slab leaks mean the integrity of your entire plumbing system is structurally compromised.

📄

Insurance Cancellation

Many Colorado homeowners are receiving notices that their home insurance is being dropped because they still have dangerous Polybutylene or Kitec piping.

The Danger of Polybutylene Pipes

The 1980s & 90s Ticking Time Bomb

If your Denver Metro home was built between the late 1970s and the mid-1990s in communities like Highlands Ranch or Parker, there is a high probability it is plumbed with Polybutylene piping (often recognizable as flexible gray plastic piping).

Over time, public water treatment chemicals (like chlorine) attack the internal structure of these plastic pipes. They become incredibly brittle from the inside out. Because they degrade internally, they give zero warning before they burst.

A ruptured Polybutylene line can flood a home in minutes, causing tens of thousands of dollars in water damage and mold remediation. A whole-home repipe using Uponor PEX-A expansion piping (backed by a 25-year material warranty and 5-year workmanship warranty) is the only way to permanently remove this risk from your property. See our successful extraction in the Highlands Ranch Case Study.

Insurance & Home Value Impact

Insurance companies are fully aware of the Polybutylene failure rate. Today, it is increasingly common for carriers to either drastically raise premiums, refuse to cover water damage, or drop home insurance policies entirely until these pipes are replaced.

Furthermore, if you ever plan to sell your home, home inspectors will flag these pipes immediately, often forcing the seller to foot the bill before closing.

The "Straw" Effect

As rust builds up inside a galvanized pipe, it thickens the inner walls, shrinking the space where water can flow. A 1-inch pipe can eventually be choked down to an opening the size of a drinking straw.

This is why turning on a washing machine causes the water pressure in your shower to drop to a trickle. No pressure-boosting valve can fix this; the pipes must be removed. Explore our historic Wash Park Case Study.

The Problem with Galvanized Steel

Rusting from the Inside Out

If you live in a historic Denver neighborhood, Arvada, or an older subdivision, your home may rely on original galvanized steel piping.

Galvanized steel was dipped in a protective zinc coating, but decades of water friction wear that coating away. Once the bare steel is exposed to water, it begins to aggressively rust and calcify.

Not only does this rust severely choke off your water pressure, but it also leaks heavy metals into the water your family drinks and bathes in. Removing these heavy, rusted pipes and replacing them with modern PEX-A or Type L Copper restores your water quality instantly. Learn about our comparison in the PEX-A vs. PEX-B Guide.

Frequently Asked Questions About Repiping

Why is Polybutylene pipe so dangerous in Colorado homes?

Polybutylene pipes react to chlorine and water treatment chemicals over time, becoming brittle and flaking from the inside out. This leads to sudden, catastrophic bursts without warning. Many Colorado insurance companies will no longer write policies for homes with these pipes.

Why does my water look yellow or rusty?

Rusty or yellow water is the primary symptom of failing galvanized steel pipes. These pipes rust from the inside out, contaminating your drinking water and restricting flow. A whole-home repipe replaces them with clean PEX or Copper.

Will a repipe increase the value of my Denver home?

Yes. Failing plumbing is a massive red flag for home inspectors and buyers. Upgrading to a Master Plumber-installed PEX-A system removes a major liability and provides a strong selling point for your property.

What type of pipe do you use for a replacement?

We highly recommend PEX Type A (Uponor). It is highly flexible, meaning it can expand during a deep Colorado freeze without bursting. It is immune to internal scaling, rusting, and corrosion. For homeowners who prefer traditional metal, we also install premium Type L copper.

Will my water be shut off for a week?

Absolutely not. A typical home repipe takes 1 to 3 days. We structure the project so that your main water is turned back on at the end of every single work day. You do not have to move into a hotel while we repipe your home.

Do you handle the drywall repair?

Yes! Unlike massive corporate plumbing companies that leave you with gaping holes in your sheetrock, Just Plumbing, Inc. hires and schedules professional drywall patchers for you. We manage the mess so you don't have to.
