Why Repipe Your Colorado Home?

★★★★☆ 4.7 stars based on 71 google reviews

Don't wait for a catastrophic ceiling collapse or dropped insurance policy. Learn the warning signs of failing plumbing and why upgrading to Uponor PEX-A or Type L Copper is the smartest decision for your home.

The 4 Critical Warning Signs of Failing Pipes

💧

Chronic Low Water Pressure

If running the washing machine turns your upstairs shower into a trickle, your pipes are clogged with decades of internal rust and calcified mineral scale.

🚰

Discolored or Metallic Water

Yellow, reddish-brown, or foul-tasting tap water means original galvanized steel lines are oxidizing and shedding heavy iron flakes directly into your drinking supply.

⚠️

Recurring Pinhole Leaks

One leak is an accident. Multiple pinholes or hot concrete slab leaks mean systemic chloramine corrosion has eaten through your copper walls everywhere.

📄

Insurance Policy Cancellation

Carriers across Colorado are refusing coverage or issuing strict non-renewal letters to homes containing polybutylene or recalled Kitec plumbing.

The Hazard of Polybutylene Pipes

The 1980s & 90s Suburban Ticking Time Bomb

If your home was built between 1978 and 1995 in communities like Highlands Ranch, Parker, or Centennial, there is a very high probability it was plumbed with gray polybutylene (Quest) tubing.

Municipal water disinfectants (chlorine and chloramines) react with polyolefin resin, causing micro-fracturing along the inner pipe walls while acetal plastic fittings turn brittle and chalky. Because the breakdown happens from the inside out, polybutylene gives zero visible warning before a high-pressure blowout.

A whole-home repipe utilizing flexible, freeze-proof Uponor PEX-A expansion tubing permanently removes this liability, backed by a 25-year material warranty and our 5-year Master Plumber workmanship guarantee. See how we resolved this in our Highlands Ranch Case Study.

Insurance Non-Renewal & Real Estate Impact

Colorado underwriters actively check for polybutylene. Insurance carriers routinely refuse to write new homeowner policies, impose steep water damage exclusions, or cancel existing coverage until the system is extracted.

During home sales, buyers' home inspectors flag polybutylene immediately, frequently jeopardizing loan underwriting. Just Plumbing provides an official Master Plumber Repipe Certificate upon project completion to clear all title contingencies.

The "Drinking Straw" Choke Effect

As mineral scale and iron rust collect inside galvanized pipe walls (tuberculation), the internal diameter shrinks relentlessly. A 3/4-inch main pipe frequently narrows down to an opening no wider than a standard drinking straw.

This constriction is why flushing a toilet drops your shower to a scalding trickle. Pressure-boosting valves cannot overcome choked pipe walls—the rusted steel must be replaced. Explore our historic transformations in the Park Hill Case Study and Wash Park Bungalow Project.

The Problem with Galvanized Steel

Internal Rusting & Heavy Metal Contamination

Homes built before 1965 in central Denver, Olde Town Arvada, Wheat Ridge, and Englewood rely heavily on original galvanized steel piping.

Galvanized steel was originally dipped in molten zinc. Decades of hot municipal water flow dissolve this zinc barrier, exposing raw iron. The bare steel rusts outward, corroding threaded fittings until they snap and release toxic iron sediment into your drinking water.

Replacing heavy, rusted pipes with smooth Uponor PEX-A or rigid Type L Copper permanently restores clean water quality, eliminates metallic odors, and doubles shower volume.

Frequently Asked Questions About Repiping

Why is Polybutylene pipe so dangerous in Colorado homes?

Polybutylene pipes react to municipal chlorine and chloramines over time, becoming brittle, flaking, and developing micro-fractures from the inside out. This leads to sudden, catastrophic bursts without warning. Many Colorado insurance companies will no longer write or renew policies for homes with these pipes.

Why does my tap water look yellow or rusty?

Rusty, brown, or discolored water is the primary symptom of failing galvanized steel pipes. As the internal zinc coating erodes, bare steel oxidizes, shedding rust and heavy metals into your drinking water while mineral scale chokes off water pressure.

Will a whole-home repipe increase the value of my Denver home?

Yes. Failing or recalled plumbing is a major red flag for home inspectors, mortgage lenders, and buyers. Upgrading to a Master Plumber-installed Uponor PEX-A or Type L Copper system removes an enormous inspection liability and boosts appraisal valuation.

What type of piping material is best for whole-home repiping?

We recommend Uponor PEX-A expansion piping. Its thermal shape memory allows it to expand up to three times its diameter during a deep Colorado freeze without splitting, contracting back when thawed. For homeowners who prefer rigid metal or historic properties, we install commercial-grade Type L Copper.

Will my water service be turned off for days during a repipe?

No. A typical single-family home repipe takes Just Plumbing just 1 to 2 business days. We isolate plumbing zones in daily phases and ensure your main water service is pressurized and restored by 5:00 PM every evening so you never have to relocate to a hotel.

Do you handle the drywall patching after repiping?

Yes! Unlike national plumbing franchises that leave you with gaping wall holes and a list of third-party contractors, Just Plumbing hires and manages a dedicated professional drywall finisher to patch, mud, and texture all plumbing access incisions to base quality.
