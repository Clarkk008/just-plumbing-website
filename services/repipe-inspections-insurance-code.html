3. Colorado Plumbing Code (IPC) & Municipal Permit Standards

Many homeowners do not realize that replacing a home's supply piping triggers mandatory International Plumbing Code (IPC) upgrades enforced by Denver, Arapahoe, Douglas, Jefferson, Adams, and Boulder County building departments. Cutting corners with an unpermitted crew leaves you exposed to code violations and denied claims:

Colorado IPC / Municipal Requirement Why It Matters for Your Home or HOA
IPC 604.8 Pressure Regulation (≤ 80 PSI) Static pressure above 80 PSI requires a calibrated brass Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) set to 60–75 PSI to protect appliances and fittings.
IPC 607.3 Closed-Loop Thermal Expansion Installing a PRV creates a closed plumbing system—code mandates a properly pressurized potable thermal expansion tank at the water heater.
100 PSI Rough-In Pressure & Dielectric Isolation Every new Uponor PEX-A or Type L Copper line is pressure-tested before walls close, with brass/dielectric transitions to prevent galvanic rust.
UL Intumescent Fire-Stopping (HOA & Commercial) Multi-family, condo, and commercial penetrations through fire-rated floors/walls must be sealed with UL-listed fire-caulk to pass marshal inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions — Inspections, Insurance & Code

Do I need a municipal plumbing permit for a whole-home repipe in Colorado?

Yes. Across Denver and Front Range municipalities, replacing a home's domestic water distribution system requires a licensed Plumbing Contractor (#PC.0003531) to pull a plumbing permit and pass a municipal pressure-test and final code inspection. Unpermitted repipes can void homeowners insurance coverage and delay future real estate closings.

What Colorado Plumbing Code (IPC) upgrades are required during a whole-home repipe?

Under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) adopted across Colorado, any property with static water pressure above 80 PSI must have a Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) set between 60 and 75 PSI paired with a thermal expansion tank on the water heater. Repipes must also pass a 100 PSI hydrostatic or air rough-in test, use dielectric isolation between dissimilar metals, and include UL-listed fire-stopping in multi-family or HOA buildings.

Can Just Plumbing inspect a home during a real estate inspection objection window and provide an underwriting certificate?

Yes. Master Plumber Bill Clark (#MP.00600120) performs rapid pre-closing pipe evaluations for buyers, sellers, and real estate brokers when general home inspectors flag polybutylene, recalled Kitec, galvanized steel, or weeping copper. Once repiped, we issue an official Master Plumber Certification packet accepted by homeowners and HOA insurance underwriters.

Will homeowners insurance drop me if I only do a partial spot repair instead of a whole-home repipe?

If your policy was flagged specifically for gray Polybutylene (PB2110) or recalled Kitec, underwriters generally require 100% extraction of the defective piping before renewing full water-damage coverage. Partial repipes and overhead slab leak bypasses are ideal when the rest of your home already has sound Type L Copper or PEX-A.
