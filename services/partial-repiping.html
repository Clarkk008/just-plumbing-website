Strategic & Focused Solutions

Whether you are dealing with brittle polybutylene sections in an accessible crawlspace, choked galvanized steel in an unfinished basement, or updating supply lines during kitchen and bath remodels, we provide precise, code-compliant integrations using flexible Uponor PEX-A (backed by a 25-year material warranty and 5-year workmanship warranty) and rigid Type L Copper.

Primary Scenarios for Partial Repiping:

  • Overhead Slab Leak Bypasses: Abandoning leaking under-slab copper lines and routing clean PEX-A overhead without jackhammering structural concrete.
  • Exposed Basement Trunks: Replacing corroded galvanized steel main trunks to restore full shower and faucet water pressure.
  • Remodeling Pre-Runs: Upgrading aging wall-cavity supply lines before custom tile, cabinetry, and drywall are installed.
  • Localized Hazard Removal: Eliminating isolated runs of polybutylene, recalled Kitec pipes, or recurring copper pinhole leaks.

The Overhead Slab Leak Alternative

Concrete slab leaks in post-war ranches across Sheridan, Englewood, and Littleton can destroy finished flooring if jackhammered. Our overhead bypass isolates the damaged underground copper line permanently, feeding new Uponor PEX-A through interior wall studs and ceiling joists—leaving your flooring untouched and your home clean.

Frequently Asked Questions About Partial Repiping

What is an overhead concrete slab leak bypass?

When a subterranean copper line ruptures underneath a concrete slab, breaking up finished flooring with jackhammers is destructive and expensive. An overhead bypass caps and abandons the leaking pipe beneath the concrete, routing flexible Uponor PEX-A expansion tubing overhead through wall studs and ceiling joists to restore water safely without floor damage.

Can you connect new PEX pipes to existing copper or galvanized lines?

Yes. Using approved transition fittings (such as dielectric unions, ProPress adapters, or threaded brass transition couplings), we safely join modern Uponor PEX-A expansion piping to existing sound copper or steel lines without causing galvanic corrosion.

Is a partial repipe enough to fix low water pressure?

If low water pressure is caused by heavy mineral rust clogging a specific main line or exposed basement manifold, replacing that key section will significantly increase flow to your fixtures. If the entire system is corroded, we may evaluate options for a whole-home repipe.

Does a partial repipe require shutting off the water for multiple days?

No. Partial repiping projects are typically completed in 1 to 2 days, and Just Plumbing, Inc. ensures water service is safely restored by the end of each workday.
