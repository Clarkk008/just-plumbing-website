Upgrading Your Home’s Plumbing Safely

If your home was built between the 1950s and early 2000s, the pipes running behind your walls are likely degrading from the inside out. At Just Plumbing, Inc., we strictly adhere to Colorado municipal building codes to map your home’s layout and install brand new, highly durable piping with precision. Need only localized sections addressed? Explore our partial repiping services.

Signs You Need A Repipe:

  • Your home has gray Polybutylene or orange/blue Kitec pipes.
  • Frequent copper pinhole leaks, damp drywall, or concrete slab leaks.
  • Rust-colored, foul-smelling, or metallic water from corroded galvanized steel.
  • Severe drops in water pressure whenever a toilet flushes or a second shower runs.

Our Targeted Repiping Solutions

Whether you are dealing with outdated legacy materials or want to upgrade to premium plumbing, we have the specialized expertise to handle it.

Polybutylene Pipe Replacement

Gray polybutylene pipes installed between 1978-1995 are well beyond their useful life. Major Colorado insurance companies are actively dropping coverage or denying claims for homes with Quest plumbing. See how we resolved this in our Highlands Ranch Case Study.

Galvanized Pipe Replacement

Older galvanized steel pipes corrode internally from mineral tuberculation, restricting water flow and releasing iron and lead particles into your drinking water. Read our real-world transformation in the Park Hill Galvanized Case Study.

Uponor PEX-A Repiping

We install premium, flexible PEX-A expansion tubing backed by a 25-year manufacturer material warranty and our 5-year workmanship warranty. Highly resistant to sub-zero freeze bursts, scale, and chloramines. Learn more in our PEX-A vs. PEX-B Guide.

Type L Copper Repiping

For homeowners who prefer traditional metal or properties in historic districts requiring all-metal construction, we install heavy-duty Type L copper. Thick walls and lead-free Master Plumber soldering ensure robust, long-term durability. Check out our Arvada Copper Repipe Case Study.

Repipe Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my house needs a full repipe?

If your home was built between the 1970s and 1990s with polybutylene pipes, between 1995 and 2007 with recalled Kitec, or prior to 1965 with galvanized steel, you are at severe risk for catastrophic leaks and insurance cancellation. Classic warning signs include chronic low water pressure, discolored rusty water, frequent pinhole leaks, and moisture damage on walls or ceilings.

Will a repipe completely destroy my drywall?

No. A professional repipe requires cuts, not demolition. We map out the exact route of your plumbing and make small, strategic incisions in the drywall. Furthermore, we actually hire and schedule a professional drywall patcher to restore your walls to base quality when the plumbing is done.

How long does a whole house repipe take?

Most standard residential repipes take between 1 to 2 business days depending on the size of the home and the number of bathrooms. We ensure you have running water at the end of each work day so you can stay in your home during the process without being displaced.

What piping materials do you install for whole-home repiping?

We install commercial-grade Uponor PEX-A (AquaPEX) expansion piping backed by a 25-year manufacturer material warranty and our 5-year Master Plumber workmanship guarantee. For mechanical rooms, high-temperature lines, and historic preservation mandates, we install rigid Type L Copper.
