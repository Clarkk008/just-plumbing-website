Historic Home Plumbing Solutions Across Denver Neighborhoods

From the classic brick bungalows of Washington Park and Congress Park to the Victorian architectural landmarks in West Highland and Park Hill, Denver's historic homes demand specialized plumbing care. These pre-1960s residences still contain original galvanized steel pipes that rust from the inside out, leading to choked water pressure, metallic-tasting water, and rust-colored stains in sinks and tubs.

The biggest fear for Denver homeowners is wall damage: standard contractors often tear through irreplaceable lath-and-plaster construction or historic woodwork to access water lines. At Just Plumbing, Inc., we utilize , minimally invasive techniques that route flexible Uponor PEX-A or rigid Type L Copper through basement ceilings and existing access voids, preserving the historic character of your home.

Preserving Denver's Historic Lath & Plaster

Standard plumbing repairs can ruin pre-war craftsmanship. Plaster walls crack easily, and historical wood moldings can be impossible to replace.

Just Plumbing's specialized repiping approach focuses on minimal penetrations. We pull fresh Uponor PEX-A expansion lines vertically through interior partition cavities without trenching through finished plaster surfaces. Your water pressure is restored to 100% capacity with zero structural devastation.

Frequently Asked Questions — Denver Plumbing

Why do historic homes in Denver suffer from low water pressure?

Homes in older Denver neighborhoods like Wash Park, Park Hill, and the Highlands typically feature original galvanized steel pipes. Over decades, minerals and rust build up inside the lines, choking off water flow until the internal opening is no wider than a straw.

Can you repipe a historic Denver home without destroying lath and plaster walls?

Yes. Just Plumbing specializes in minimally invasive repiping techniques. By using flexible Uponor PEX-A expansion lines, we can fish modern piping through basements, crawlspaces, and existing stud bays, making tiny incisions rather than demolishing historic lath and plaster.

Does repiping or water heater installation require a permit in Denver?

Yes. All whole-home repiping and water heater installations require permits and inspections from the City and County of Denver Community Planning and Development Department. Operating under Colorado Contractor Registration #PC.0003531, Just Plumbing pulls all required permits.
