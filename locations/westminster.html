Solving Westminster's Aging Pipes & Drainage Demands

Spanning across Adams and Jefferson counties, Westminster contains two distinct eras of home construction with divergent plumbing challenges. Older neighborhoods near Historic Westminster and 72nd Avenue contain original galvanized steel pipes that have choked water pressure down to single-fixture capacity. In contrast, 1980s and 1990s subdivisions surrounding Standley Lake, Legacy Ridge, and Countryside were plumbed with defective polybutylene plastic.

Additionally, properties situated near the Big Dry Creek basin, Farmers' High Line Canal, and Standley Lake sit over active clay soils and shallow water tables. During severe spring downpours, a reliable sump pump and battery backup system is essential to protect finished basements from destructive groundwater seepage.

Frequently Asked Questions — Westminster Plumbing

What are the common pipe problems in Westminster, CO homes?

Westminster features a dual plumbing profile. In south and historic Westminster, pre-1970s homes frequently suffer from rusted, choked galvanized steel or thin copper lines. In 1980s and 1990s developments around Standley Lake and Legacy Ridge, homes frequently contain defective polybutylene pipes that cause sudden wall leaks and homeowner insurance non-renewals.

Why are basement sump pumps important in Westminster?

Properties surrounding Standley Lake, Farmers' High Line Canal, and Big Dry Creek sit over fluctuating water tables and clay soils. High-capacity cast-iron sump pumps equipped with dual-battery backup systems are essential to prevent basement slab flooding during heavy spring snowmelts and severe thunderstorms.

Are plumbing permits required for repiping and water heaters in Westminster?

Yes. The City of Westminster Building Division requires permits and inspections for all whole-home repiping and water heater replacements. Just Plumbing, Inc. holds Colorado Plumbing Contractor Registration #PC.0003531 and coordinates all municipal filings and final inspections.
