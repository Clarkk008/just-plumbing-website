PEX-A vs. PEX-B: What Every Homeowner Must Know

★★★★☆ 4.7 stars based on 71 google reviews

Not all PEX tubing is created equal. Master Plumber Bill Clark explains the critical differences in manufacturing, water flow restrictions, connection methods, and freeze tolerance between PEX-A and PEX-B.

The PEX Dilemma: Premium Expansion vs. Budget Crimp

When receiving quotes for a whole-home repipe across Denver or the surrounding Front Range, many homeowners are told simply that the contractor will install "PEX pipe." However, the letters A and B refer to drastically different chemical manufacturing processes, connection types, and performance capabilities. While low-bid contractors rely on budget PEX-B to save on material costs, Just Plumbing, Inc. exclusively installs Uponor PEX-A.

The 4 Decisive Differences Between PEX-A and PEX-B

1. Connection Design: Full-Port Expansion vs. Restricted Crimp

The most substantial difference in daily home performance is hydraulic flow. PEX-B requires inserting an undersized fitting into the pipe, which is secured by crushing an external copper ring or stainless steel clamp over the joint. This insert fitting reduces the internal diameter by 20% to 30% at every turn, tee, and fixture connection. In contrast, PEX-A utilizes ASTM F1960 ProPEX cold-expansion fittings: the pipe and collar are temporarily expanded, a full-diameter fitting is inserted, and the pipe shrinks back to create a permanent seal. Water flows through PEX-A with zero restriction, eliminating the shower pressure drops common in PEX-B systems.

2. Cross-Linking Chemistry & Molecular Flexibility

PEX-A is manufactured using the Engel (peroxide) method, which cross-links polyethylene molecules while in an amorphous, molten state. This yields a cross-linking density exceeding 85%, resulting in exceptional flexibility and thermal memory. PEX-B is manufactured using the Silane (moisture-cure) method, where cross-linking occurs after extrusion, reaching only 65% to 70% density. PEX-B is noticeably stiffer, harder to route through tight wall cavities, and retains coil memory that resists clean installation.

3. Kink Recovery vs. Pipe Replacement

If a plumber accidentally kinks a PEX-B pipe while pulling it through a wall stud, the molecular structure at the fold is permanently cracked and weakened. That section must be cut out and repaired with two additional crimp fittings—adding failure points inside your wall. If PEX-A kinks, its thermal shape memory allows it to be heated gently with a hot-air gun. The polymer flows back to its original factory shape and molecular strength, requiring zero cuts or concealed joints.

4. Sub-Zero Freeze-Burst Tolerance

In Colorado's alpine climate, water lines routed through unconditioned crawlspaces or exterior overhangs face severe freeze risks. When water turns to ice, it expands by approximately 9%. PEX-A can expand up to 300% of its normal diameter without splitting, then contracts back to factory tolerances as it thaws. PEX-B has limited elasticity; under severe freeze cycles, the rigid mechanical crimp rings and stiffer pipe walls frequently split, causing burst lines during winter thaws.

Frequently Asked Questions — PEX Comparison

Is PEX-A better than PEX-B for whole-home repiping?

Yes. PEX-A has a higher degree of cross-linking (over 85%), giving it molecular shape memory, superior flexibility, and the ability to expand when frozen without splitting. Most importantly, PEX-A utilizes full-port expansion fittings that do not restrict water pressure, whereas PEX-B relies on insert crimp fittings that choke pipe volume by 20% to 30%.

Why does PEX-B restrict water flow and pressure?

PEX-B connections require inserting a smaller fitting inside the pipe before securing it with an external copper or stainless steel crimp ring. Because the fitting fits inside the pipe, the internal diameter drops significantly at every turn, tee, and valve, causing noticeable pressure loss when multiple plumbing fixtures run at once.

What happens if a PEX-B pipe kinks during installation?

When PEX-B kinks, its crystalline molecular structure permanently weakens at the crease. The damaged section must be cut out entirely and spliced with two additional crimp fittings. Conversely, PEX-A features thermal memory—a kink can be completely repaired using a controlled heat gun, returning the pipe to its full structural integrity without extra joints.

Why do some contractors install PEX-B instead of PEX-A?

Cost is the primary factor. PEX-B tubing, manual crimp tools, and budget crimp rings are considerably cheaper to purchase than commercial PEX-A expansion systems. Low-bid plumbers use PEX-B to reduce overhead, but homeowners pay the long-term price in reduced water pressure and higher freeze risk.
