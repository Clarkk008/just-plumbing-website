PEX-A vs. PEX-B: What's The Difference?

Not all PEX piping is created equal. Learn why Master Plumbers use expansion PEX-A over crimp-style PEX-B.

Understanding connection types, water flow rates, and freeze tolerance will save you from low shower pressure and premature joint leaks.

Get a Free PEX-A Estimate

Direct Comparison: PEX-A vs. PEX-B

Feature PEX-A (Uponor / ProPEX) PEX-B (Hardware Crimp)
Fitting Method Cold Expansion (Full port) Crimp / Clamp Rings (Insert fittings)
Water Flow / Pressure 100% Full Flow (Zero restriction) 20–30% flow reduction at every fitting
Cross-Linking Method Engel Method (85%+ cross-linking) Silane Method (65–70% cross-linking)
Freeze Burst Resistance High (Expands & returns to shape) Moderate (Rigid wall can split)
Kink Repair Heatable with heat gun to restore shape Must cut pipe & insert coupling

1. The Flow Rate Bottleneck (Expansion vs. Crimp)

While PEX-B utilizes crimp rings that neck down inside the fitting—reducing water volume and restricting household flow rates—the cold-expansion design of PEX-A maintains a full internal diameter for peak water pressure. That fundamental performance difference is why we use Uponor PEX-A exclusively for every residential repiping and whole-house upgrade project across the Denver Metro Area.

2. Thermal Shape Memory in Colorado Winters

Because PEX-A is cross-linked during the raw resin extrusion stage (Engel method), its molecules form a continuous three-dimensional grid. When water freezes and expands inside PEX-A, the pipe expands naturally with the ice and contracts to its exact original shape without structural weakening.

3. Replacing Failing Plumbing Systems

When homeowners experience sudden leaks in brittle polybutylene lines, choked volume in corroded galvanized steel, dezincification in Kitec systems, or sub-surface copper pinhole leaks, installing PEX-B crimp lines only creates new flow restrictions. Upgrading with full-port PEX-A permanently restores reliable water flow.

4. Why Low-Cost Contractors Use PEX-B

PEX-B is cheaper to produce and install with inexpensive hand tools. Low-bid plumbers use PEX-B to cut costs on materials.
