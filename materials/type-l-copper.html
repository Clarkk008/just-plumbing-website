Type L Hard Copper Piping Systems

The Enduring Standard of Commercial-Grade Type L Copper

While Uponor PEX-A expansion piping is our flagship recommendation for whole-home residential repiping due to its flexibility and sub-zero freeze protection, rigid copper remains an indispensable material in high-performance plumbing. However, the grade of copper makes all the difference. Many residential builders install thin, budget-grade Type M copper to cut costs. At Just Plumbing, Inc., we refuse to install Type M. We exclusively install heavy-wall Type L Hard Copper.

Type L vs. Type M: Why Wall Thickness Matters

Copper tubing is manufactured in three primary ASTM B88 standard wall thicknesses: Type K (thickest, used for underground water service lines), Type L (medium thickness, commercial standard), and Type M (thinnest, residential budget standard):

1. 30% to 50% Thicker Protective Walls

A 3/4-inch Type L copper pipe features a wall thickness of 0.045 inches, compared to just 0.032 inches for Type M. That additional metal density provides critical resistance against localized pinhole pitting corrosion caused by municipal chloramines and Front Range water chemistry swings, extending the operational life of the installation by decades.

2. High-Pressure & Surge Tolerance

Type L copper possesses a rated working pressure of over 500 PSI at room temperature—more than double the burst margin of thin-wall metal. In communities with elevated municipal supply pressures like Lone Tree, Castle Rock, and Golden, Type L copper easily handles hydraulic surges and thermal expansion without joint fatigue.

3. Structural Rigidity for Mechanical Rooms

PEX tubing can sag or vibrate when exposed to high-velocity water movement. Type L copper provides straight, rigid, self-supporting pipe runs that cleanly anchor to mechanical room walls, delivering clean, vibration-free water lines around boilers, water heaters, and recirculating pumps.

When We Recommend Type L Copper Over PEX-A

While Uponor PEX-A is ideal for routing through finished walls, Type L Copper is the clear engineering choice in several critical applications:

  • Water Heater Transitions: Building codes require metallic piping within the first 18 inches of standard atmospheric gas water heaters to withstand high flue-gas temperatures.
  • High-Temperature Hydronic Boilers: Baseboard heating and closed hydronic boiler loops operating at 180°F+ demand rigid copper to eliminate oxygen permeation and handle thermal loads.
  • Historic Landmark Preservation: Historic districts across Denver (such as Park Hill, Baker, and Country Club) often mandate all-metal plumbing systems to maintain period architectural integrity.
  • Commercial Risers & Multi-Family Chases: Multi-story vertical risers in Glendale and Denver commercial properties frequently specify rigid Type L copper for fire safety ratings and structural rigidity.

Master Plumber Precision: Deburring & Velocity Control

Copper pinhole leaks are rarely caused by defective metal—they are caused by poor installation. When untrained installers cut copper pipe with a roller cutter and fail to ream the inside edge, a sharp internal ridge remains.

This ridge creates localized micro-turbulence that scours the protective copper oxide layer, causing erosion-corrosion pinholes right downstream of the fitting. Bill Clark's team thoroughly deburrs and reams every single cut, sizes lines to keep flow velocity below 5 feet per second, and uses precision lead-free solder or Viega ProPress mechanical fittings for flawless joint integrity.

Frequently Asked Questions — Type L Copper

What is the difference between Type L and Type M copper pipe?

The primary difference is wall thickness. Type L copper (identified by blue print) has roughly 30% to 50% thicker walls than thin residential Type M copper (identified by red print). That extra wall thickness provides superior pressure tolerance, greater resistance to pitting corrosion, and significantly longer operational lifespans.

When should a homeowner choose Type L Copper over Uponor PEX-A?

Type L copper is required in exposed mechanical rooms, direct water heater hookups within the first 18 inches, high-temperature hydronic boiler loops, and historic properties where local historic landmark preservation codes or HOA bylaws mandate all-metallic supply systems.

How does Just Plumbing prevent pinhole leaks when installing Type L copper?

We eliminate the primary causes of copper failure by properly sizing main lines to maintain water velocity under 5 feet per second, thoroughly deburring and reaming all pipe cuts to eliminate internal turbulence, installing calibrated pressure reducing valves (PRVs) below 75 PSI, and using dielectric unions to isolate dissimilar metals.

Can PEX-A and Type L Copper be combined in the same home?

Yes. This hybrid configuration is very common. We often install rigid Type L copper for the main water service entry, water heater connections, and exposed mechanical room manifolds, then transition to flexible Uponor PEX-A for distribution lines running through walls and ceiling joists to individual fixtures.
